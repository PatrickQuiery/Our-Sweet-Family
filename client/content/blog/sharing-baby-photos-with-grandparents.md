Every new parent knows the feeling: your phone is full of photos the grandparents would *love* to see, but the only "easy" way to share them feels wrong. Posting to Facebook or Instagram means broadcasting your child to the whole internet. Texting photos one at a time is tedious and they get lost in the thread. And a shared cloud album is either locked to one brand of phone or a confusing tangle of links and logins.

Here's a calmer approach.

## The problem with the usual options

- **Social media** turns your child into public (or semi-public) content, feeds ad-targeting, and lives on servers you don't control.
- **Group texts** bury photos in chat history and compress them into mush.
- **Generic cloud albums** assume everyone uses the same ecosystem and are fiddly for less tech-savvy relatives.

What most families actually want is narrow and simple: *let a specific list of people see new photos of my kids, and nothing else.*

## What to look for instead

1. **Private by default.** No public links, no discoverability, no ads. The photos should only be visible to people you personally invite.
2. **Dead-simple for grandparents.** They should be able to tap one link, and see the photos — without creating passwords they'll forget or learning a new app.
3. **Per-person control.** You decide who sees what. Aunt Kate can see everyone; the family friend only sees your oldest.
4. **A real home, not a feed.** Photos organized by child and date, so a grandparent can actually find "Emma's first birthday" later.

## The one-time setup that saves you months

Spend ten minutes once:

- Create a private family space and add your children.
- Invite the grandparents and close relatives with a single link each.
- Upload a batch of your favorite recent photos and videos.

From then on, sharing is a single upload — and everyone you invited sees it, privately, on their own time.

## Bonus: they'll see how old your child was

One small thing that delights grandparents: when each photo automatically shows your child's age at the time ("14 months"), a scroll through the year becomes a little story instead of a pile of pictures.
