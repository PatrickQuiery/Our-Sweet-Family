Most of us want the same two things that feel like they're in tension: we want the people who love our kids to see them grow up, and we don't want our children's faces, names, and birthdays scattered permanently across the public internet.

You can have both. It just takes being a little more deliberate about *where* the photos go.

## Why "just posting them" is riskier than it feels

- **It's permanent and public.** A photo posted today can be copied, cached, and indexed in ways you can't undo — years before your child is old enough to have any say.
- **It feeds ad-targeting.** On ad-supported platforms, your photos and the data around them are the product.
- **Metadata leaks more than the picture.** Photos can carry location and timing data, and a birthday caption plus a school jersey tells a stranger a lot.

None of this means you have to stop sharing. It means choosing tools designed to keep sharing *contained*.

## A simple privacy checklist

1. **Share to people, not to the public.** Use something where photos are visible only to a list you control — not to "friends of friends" or anyone with a link.
2. **Avoid public links entirely.** The safest link is one that doesn't exist. Prefer services that stream photos only to signed-in, authorized people.
3. **Turn off location on shared photos**, or use a service that doesn't expose it.
4. **Keep the sensitive stuff separate.** Bath-time and medical photos, if you keep them at all, should be locked down tighter than a birthday party.
5. **Know the exit.** Can you export your originals and delete everything if you change your mind? If not, that's a red flag.

## The mental model that helps

Think of your children's photos like a family photo album, not a bulletin board. An album is something you *hand to specific people*. A bulletin board is something you *post for anyone walking by*. Almost everything about "sharenting" goes wrong when we treat the second like the first.

## What good privacy looks like in practice

- Photos stored privately and served only to people you invited, after an access check.
- No public URLs, no ads, no selling of data.
- Per-child, per-person controls so each relative sees exactly what you intend.
- The ability to export and delete on your terms.
