The first year is a blur — a wonderful, sleep-deprived blur. You'll take thousands of photos, and if you're like most parents, almost all of them will sit unsorted in your camera roll forever. This is a gentle checklist of moments worth capturing, plus one small habit that keeps them from disappearing.

## The milestones

- **Coming home** — those first minutes in the door.
- **First bath**, first real smile, first laugh.
- **Rolling over, sitting up, crawling, first steps.**
- **First foods** (the faces alone are worth it).
- **First tooth**, first words, first haircut.
- **Monthly photo in the same spot** — same chair or blanket each month makes a growth story you'll treasure.

## The everyday moments you'll miss most

The milestones are easy to remember to shoot. It's the *ordinary* ones that hit hardest later:

- The way they fall asleep on someone's chest.
- Tiny hands and feet, close up.
- Bath time, bedtime, the morning stretch.
- The unglamorous stuff — the high chair mess, the car seat nap.
- **You, in the photos too.** Hand the camera over. Your child will want to see you there.

## Capture a little video, not just stills

A ten-second clip of a baby's laugh or babble will move you more in five years than any perfect portrait. Shoot short videos often; you don't need to film everything, just the sounds.

## The one habit that saves the year

Here's the part that matters more than any shot list: **do a five-minute weekly cleanup.** Once a week, pick your favorites from the week, put them somewhere organized, and let the rest stay in the camera roll. That's it.

Why weekly? Because "I'll organize it all later" becomes "I have 11,000 photos and I'll never touch them." A small weekly ritual keeps the good ones findable — by month, by milestone, by child.

## Make them easy to look back on

Photos you can't find might as well not exist. The best system is one where:

- Everything is grouped by child and date automatically.
- Each photo shows how old your baby was.
- The grandparents can see them too, without you lifting a finger twice.
